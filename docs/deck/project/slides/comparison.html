No TCP in the Aeron path. Aeron sends over UDP with its own lightweight reliability: no connection state, no per-segment acknowledgements, no congestion control or retransmit timers; its figures are the NIC, the fabric and the library. A FIX session runs on TCP, so our path also pays a TCP stack: ≈ 3 to 5.5 µs one way for FreeBSD's on F-Stack, more on the kernel, plus the engines' own ≈ 2 to 3.7 µs.

Reading it. No published study measures a FIX engine between two AWS hosts with its journal on. Our DPDK floor on c6in.2xlarge, ≈ 11.4 µs one way, matches Aeron Premium's transport-only figure on a host eight times larger; on AMD metal the full FIX path is ≈ 2 µs above it.

Buyers will ask how this compares with what is out there. The honest answer is that nothing published is the same measurement: AWS's own benchmarks are for Aeron, a UDP messaging library with no TCP stack, no session layer and no journal, on a much larger instance, and the AWS exchange prototype includes a matching engine. Read against those, our numbers sit at the level of the best transport-only results while carrying a full FIX session, a TCP stack and durable persistence.
