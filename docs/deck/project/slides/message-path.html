One order, one ack

Twelve stages on one thread each side; the engines' share is ≈ 2 to 3.7 µs per direction (4 to 7.5 per round trip)

Send path (initiator), 1.9 µs on F-Stack, 3.8 on the kernel

1 claim ring space

2 encode in place

3 commit

4 journal write

5 socket write

6 on the wire

Receive to ack (simulator), 4.1 µs on F-Stack, 7.0 on the kernel

7 socket read

8 frame and parse

9 session checks

10 journal inbound

11 handler, book

12 ack: 1 to 5 again

The order is sent from the loop thread, so stages 1 to 5 run back to back with no hand-off. The socket write is the largest fixed cost: 2.5 µs per message on the kernel, 0.76 on F-Stack. The journal writes cost 0.4 to 0.8 µs each after the page-fault fix.

On receive, up to 2,048 bytes are read in one call, the flyweight is pointed at them, sequence and session state are checked, the bytes are journaled, and the handler writes the ExecutionReport through the same five send stages. No object is created on either side.

Stage times from the per-stage timestamps on c6in.2xlarge; in-process total ≈ 3.7 µs per direction on F-Stack, 6.2 on the kernel (7.4 and 12.3 per round trip)

This is the life of one order and its acknowledgement. Each side runs its stages on the one event-loop thread. The orange boxes are the journal writes, which stay on the critical path by design. The dark boxes are where the bytes meet the transport, and that is where the F-Stack transport saves most of its time.
